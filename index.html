5.1 Blackbody Radiation

Notes (Classwork + Homework)

Classical physics predicted Rayleigh-Jeans law \( u(\lambda) \propto T/\lambda^4\) which diverges at short λ - Ultraviolet catastrophe.

Planck (1900) proposed energy is quantized: \( E = nh\nu \). This solved blackbody spectrum.

Planck's Law: \( B_{\lambda}(T) = \frac{2hc^2}{\lambda^5} \frac{1}{e^{hc/\lambda kT}-1} \)
Wien's Law: \( \lambda_{max} T = 2.898 \times 10^{-3} m\cdot K \)
Stefan-Boltzmann: \( E = \sigma T^4 \)
Derivation Idea: Planck assumed oscillators in cavity can have only \(E_n=n h\nu\). Average energy \(\langle E\rangle = \frac{h\nu}{e^{h\nu/kT}-1}\) not \(kT\). Insert into mode density \(8\pi\nu^2/c^3\) gives Planck law.

5.2 The Photoelectric Effect

Notes

Light ejects electrons only if \(h\nu > \Phi\). Kinetic energy independent of intensity, depends on frequency - proves particle nature of light (Einstein 1905).

Einstein: \( K_{max}=h\nu - \Phi = h\nu - h\nu_0 \)
\( eV_0 = K_{max} \)
Derivation: Photon gives energy hν to electron. Work function Φ used to escape. Remainder = KE. At threshold ν₀, K=0 => Φ=hν₀. Stopping potential V₀ stops electrons: ½mv² = eV₀.

5.3 The Compton Effect

Notes

X-ray photon collides with electron, wavelength increases. Proves photon has momentum \(p=h/\lambda\).

\( \Delta\lambda = \lambda' - \lambda = \frac{h}{m_e c}(1-\cos\theta) = \lambda_c(1-\cos\theta)\)
Compton wavelength \(\lambda_c = 2.426 \times 10^{-12} m\)
Derivation: Use conservation of energy and momentum: photon \(E=pc\), electron relativistic \(E^2=(pc)^2+(m_ec^2)^2\). Solving for λ' gives shift. Derivation steps show elastic collision of particle-like photon.

5.4 De Broglie's Matter Wave

Notes

If light (wave) can be particle, particles can be waves. de Broglie 1924: Every moving particle has wavelength.

\( \lambda = \frac{h}{p} = \frac{h}{mv} = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2mK}} \) for non-relativistic
For electron accelerated by V: \( \lambda = \frac{1.227}{\sqrt{V}} nm \)
Derivation: Start from \(E=mc^2\) and \(E=h\nu\), momentum of photon \(p=E/c=h/\lambda\). Postulate same for matter: λ=h/p. For electron in Bohr orbit, standing wave condition \(2πr=nλ\) gives Bohr quantization \(L=nħ\).

5.5 Wave-Particle Duality

Concept: Light and matter exhibit both wave (interference, diffraction) and particle (photoelectric, Compton) properties. Complementarity principle (Bohr): We see one aspect at a time depending on experiment.

Wave → \( \lambda, \nu, interference \)
Particle → \( E=h\nu, p=h/\lambda, collision \)
Critical Thinking: Not object is both, but quantum object needs new description - wavefunction ψ gives probability. Intensity of light (wave) ∝ probability of finding photon (particle). I analyzed: blackbody + photoelectric + Compton need particle, but double-slit needs wave - duality resolves crisis.

5.6 Electron Double-Slit Experiment

Notes

Even single electrons fired one by one build interference pattern - each electron interferes with itself. Probability wave.

Position of maxima: \( d \sin\theta = m\lambda \)
Intensity: \( I = 4I_0 \cos^2(\frac{\pi d \sin\theta}{\lambda}) \cdot sinc^2 \)

Learning: If we watch which slit, interference disappears - measurement collapses wavefunction.

5.7 Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle

Notes + Simple Consequences

Cannot know position and momentum simultaneously precisely. Arises from wave nature.

\( \Delta x \Delta p_x \ge \frac{\hbar}{2} \approx \frac{h}{4\pi} \)
\( \Delta E \Delta t \ge \frac{\hbar}{2} \)
Derivation Idea: Wave packet width Δx ~ 1/Δk. Since p=ħk, ΔxΔp ~ ħ. More rigorous from Fourier transform of ψ and standard deviation.
Consequences: 1) Electron cannot be in nucleus (Δx~10⁻¹⁴m → huge Δp → huge KE). 2) Zero-point energy - even at 0K, harmonic oscillator has E₀=½ħω. 3) Tunneling allowed due to ΔE.
